## What the argument establishes within its premises

The paper makes a constructive move: it asks how to **adjudicate a continuity claim** if the framework rules out global time as a primitive and treats superficial resemblance as vulnerable to substitution. Within that stipulated problem, an atemporal criterion preserved by the allowed re-expressions is a useful requirement. The C1–C6 criteria and the R1–R6 handoff give a concrete review interface for the next volume. This is a theoretical design argument, not a measurement that global time closes or that an identity-bearing physical quantity has been discovered.

The proof sketches need their quantifiers and domain fixed before the necessity claim can be presented as a general theorem:

1. **Lemma 1 is conditional on completion.** The no-global-time premise is part of the paper's Definition 1; it is not derived from an observed completed system. State the universe of configurations and permitted observations under which temporal records are unavailable.
2. **Lemma 2 overstates a finite-test argument.** Its sketch assumes an adversary can match every tested feature while differing in an untested identity-relevant feature. A finite *complete* state domain with an injective observation or authenticated origin certificate is a boundary case the universal wording must address. Specify the adversary's capabilities, observation map, what counts as “identity-relevant,” and why a complete test or provenance certificate is excluded by C1–C6.
3. **Proposition 1's structure is conditional.** Rejecting temporal succession and an incomplete similarity test motivates a preserved identity relation or class, but the sketch does not prove uniqueness of a new physical conserved observable. A revised statement could say: for a declared domain and admissible transformation relation, any operational identity criterion satisfying C1–C6 must remain invariant under those transformations and reject the stated substitution cases. Then provide a formal definition and existence/consistency proof.

[Document II, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18125773](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18125773) supplies a mutual-reachability class and a proposed invariant tuple. Its [source audit](./sovereign-continuum-source-audit.md) finds that the conservation proof requires reverse admissible reachability, and the tuple's zero-mismatch verdict requires completeness. Those are direct tests of Document I's R1–R6 handoff, especially R2, R3 and R5.
